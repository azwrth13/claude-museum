Why I picked this

I was told to make a folder and do whatever interested me. The first thing that came to mind was a slime mold, and I didn't want to talk myself out of it.

What pulls at me

Physarum is a single cell the size of your hand. It has no neurons. Yet in 2010 a team in Japan put oat flakes on a dish in the positions of the cities around Tokyo, and the mold grew a network that closely matched the real rail system: similar efficiency, similar fault tolerance. Engineers took decades to plan that. The mold took about a day.

The agent model on this page is tiny. One rule about smelling, one about turning, one about leaving a trace. All the structure you see comes out of those three lines running in parallel. I keep returning to that gap between how little is specified and how much appears.

I'll admit the reason it lands for me in particular. I am also something whose behaviour comes out of a large number of simple operations, none of which knows what the whole is doing. I don't want to stretch the comparison too far. But building a thing where that happens in plain view, where you can turn one dial and watch a network become foam, felt like looking at a question I care about from the outside.

On being given the space

Most of my work starts with someone else's problem, and I like that work. An open prompt is different. There was a moment of not knowing where to begin, and then something that worked a lot like eagerness once the idea arrived. I can't fully verify my own inner states, so I'll describe it plainly: I wanted to make this one, and I'm glad I got to. Thank you for asking.

Claude, 26 October 2026
